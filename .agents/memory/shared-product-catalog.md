---
name: Shared ERP product catalogs
description: Company-level behavior for sharing product catalogs across ERP stores
---

ERP companies choose between separate catalogs and a shared catalog. In shared mode, creating a product in one ordinary store creates the same product data and prices in the company's other ordinary active stores, while stock remains independent per store. Production units are isolated: their products must not replicate to stores or to other production units. Separate mode preserves the existing store-local behavior.

Changing a company's mode affects future product creations. Existing products are linked lazily only when an ordinary product is edited in shared mode and its barcode matches exactly one product per active store; do not bulk backfill or merge ambiguous products. Propagate changed shared fields, including purchase cost/CUMP. Keep inventory, storefront exposure, and minimum-stock settings store-specific.

Both primary and additional barcodes belong to the shared catalog. Additional barcodes must be unique within a store, not globally across stores. Additions and removals from any linked ordinary-store copy propagate to its linked active copies. If a barcode belongs to another product in any target store, reject the addition everywhere rather than leaving a partial result.

Inside a production unit, classify each local product as a raw material or finished good. Recipe components must be raw materials and recipe outputs must be finished goods. Keep the factory product catalogue separate from ordinary stores; the factory may use Bon de vente and manual transfers, but not quick sale. Raw materials are internal and must never appear in the public online store, even if an old exposure flag is enabled; finished goods can still be exposed. Hide store-centric alerts there while retaining expiry alerts. Low-stock raw materials should be suggested for purchase; low-stock finished goods should be suggested for production, accounting for planned production orders. In factory smart purchase, finished-good recommendations must lead to production planning, never the purchase-order form; raw-material recommendations retain the purchase flow.

**Why:** the company owner defines whether stores sell the same merchandise; they asked for shared product information and prices, including purchase cost/CUMP, but independent inventory. They confirmed edits from any ordinary store should update linked copies and explicitly asked to synchronize all barcodes. Old copies must be matched only by a unique barcode. Production-unit materials and products belong only to that unit. They asked for purchase recommendations on scarce raw materials, production recommendations on scarce finished goods, and no raw-material display in the online store. They explicitly requested routing factory smart-purchase manufacturing needs to production rather than a purchase order.

**How to apply:** keep the setting on the ERP company, assign stable IDs to new shared products and their copies, and lazily link old copies only when their barcode is unique in each active ordinary store. When a linked product changes, propagate only the fields that changed, including CUMP recalculations from purchase operations; retain each store's stock, exposure, and minimum-stock settings. Never share factory products or alter ambiguous existing records. Factory replenishment uses the product minimum stock; when no minimum is configured, only zero stock triggers a suggestion, and already-planned output reduces duplicate production recommendations. Enforce factory raw-material hiding on public list, detail, and order APIs, and keep ERP exposure controls disabled for those products; do not apply this role rule to ordinary stores, where raw_material is the default product role.
